**Founder discussion note — 1 November 2026.** Proposed positioning, not an

## Assessment today

LearnAI has an implemented tutoring-engine foundation and a coherent product
direction. It does **not yet have a demonstrated competitive advantage** in
learning outcomes, customer acquisition, curriculum coverage, usability or cost.
The full persistent student product is still to be built.

Elo, logistic prediction, skill graphs, spaced retrieval and access to foundation
models are available techniques. Combining them is useful engineering, but does
not establish uniqueness. Provider independence, explicit evidence policies and
replayable evaluation can support reliable execution; buyers need a measurable
benefit from those choices.

## A positioning hypothesis worth testing

**Help learners become independently capable, with progress evidence that keeps
track of assistance and checks whether learning lasts.**

Make that promise concrete for one student group, curriculum/language and
learning moment. Possible advantages to earn include easier mathematics input,
better support for a neglected curriculum, or a workflow that saves a particular
tutoring organisation work. None of those gaps has been established yet.

The present foundation includes supported verification, revision-aware tutoring,
assistance-aware evidence and evaluation traces. Durable cross-session learning,
the complete Review scheduler, broad curriculum coverage and meaningful
within-skill difficulty variation remain ahead. Avoid presenting the intended
learning loop as fully operational.

## Answering “Why wouldn't we build it ourselves?”

This question mainly concerns organisations with development capacity; a family
or student is more likely to ask why they should choose us over another product.
These are two separate comparisons: **buy versus build**, and **LearnAI versus
other vendors**. A good answer to the first does not settle the second.

A candid answer to develop through pilots:

> You could build it. Our job is to make a maintained learning system more
> valuable than building and operating one yourselves. That means useful content,
> dependable assessment, appropriate tutoring, progress tracking and ongoing
> quality work. We should earn your decision through a pilot that demonstrates
> learning value, fit with your workflow and the total effort required.

The engineering burden spans content authoring and review, answer verification,
skill/evidence modelling, calibration, maths input, data persistence, integrations,
evaluation after model changes, monitoring and support. Much of that is ongoing
work. This is a potential reason to buy, not a claim that LearnAI already delivers
all of it or that an in-house build necessarily costs more.

Building may be sensible when the buyer has strong capabilities, unusual control
requirements, or regards the learning system as its own core product. We should
qualify those cases honestly. This discussion does not commit us to an API,
white-label offering, school sales or any other business model.

## What could become defensible

- Reviewed, curriculum-specific content and calibration that measurably improve
  the chosen learning experience.
- A repeatable quality process and evidence of independently completed work
  after a delay, supported by appropriately handled learner data.
- A usable product and trusted relationships in a specific market, with a
  distribution channel and operating model that work.

These assets may compound with use, but raw interaction volume alone is not an
advantage. We need permission to use data, informative observations and evidence
that changes actually improve outcomes. No proprietary-data advantage is claimed
today.

## Next discovery and validation work

1. Pick a candidate buyer and learner problem; observe their current workflow and
   compare the alternatives actually available for their curriculum and language.
2. Test the experience against a relevant incumbent or the buyer's existing
   approach. Assess usability, independent/delayed performance and support burden.
3. Establish build-versus-buy assumptions with the buyer: implementation effort,
   content maintenance, integrations, serving cost and recurring operations.
4. Set pilot criteria in advance. Claim better outcomes, lower cost or faster
   deployment only when the comparison supports them.

The immediate objective is evidence of a reason to choose LearnAI in one market.
Broad subject coverage is a long-term vision, not a substitute for that evidence.
